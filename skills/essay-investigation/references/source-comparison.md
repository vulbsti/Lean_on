## The change in the work

The first essay separately explained cultural interpretations of feelings, historical romantic traditions, idealization, storytelling, and long-term compatibility. Those distinctions were individually intelligible, but the draft spent too much time establishing familiar propositions.

The revision proposed a question about a possible systematic mismatch: could cultural models favor the experience of entering a relationship over the experience of sustaining one? History, storytelling incentives, psychological expectations, and consequences now contributed to one explanation.

Its proposed mechanism was that uncertainty and obstacles make compelling stories; those stories may circulate successfully; audiences may internalize their expectations; those expectations may shape choices. This is an example of a mechanism to investigate, not a verified account of romantic culture.

The revision's strongest objection also changed the question. Perhaps people understand the limits of initial attraction and knowingly value immediate experience. If so, the apparent mistake may be a trade-off between preferences at different times rather than cultural deception. An objection earned its place by changing the explanation.

The final response named the process **Observation → Hypothesis → Mechanism → Investigation → Synthesis**, expanded into finding the problem, forming a risky hypothesis, explaining the mechanism, constructing the argument, attacking the hypothesis, and compressing the draft.

## Limits to preserve

The chat itself identified unresolved weaknesses: historical transmission was not established; a plausible narrative-selection mechanism did not prove its importance; hypothetical relationship trajectories were not general empirical findings; and the provocative title implied more design and certainty than the argument supported.

The inspected framework and revised essay were returned without truncation. The tool capped the first essay at 20,000 characters, so its ending was not inspected. The comparison rests on the visible draft, the user's explicit verdicts, the complete revision, and the complete framework. The model's retrospective description of “how it was thinking” is a proposed account of writing strategy, not an independently verified record of its internal process.

The skill operationalizes the framework with claim-linked research notes, local qualifications, and dependency-based revision. Those are implementation choices serving the framework, not additional user quotations. No factual romance thesis, mandatory section count, author imitation, or claim of independently tested writing quality is carried forward.

The next useful validation is an essay on a different subject, judged as a complete reading experience: does it develop a consequential question, let evidence and objections alter the answer, and reach an insight that the notes did not already contain? Structural validation alone cannot answer that.
