---
name: investigative-essay-chatgpt
description: Develop or revise a reflective, idea-led essay from a conversation, notes, or a draft when the author wants readers to follow a difficult question in plain language.
---

Find the point where the author's first answer stopped satisfying them. In a conversation this often occurs after everyone seems to agree. Recover the original expectation, the observation that disturbed it, the rival explanation, and what deciding between them would change. Work privately unless the author asks to see the reasoning or outline. Ask a focused question only if the missing answer would materially change the piece.

Build the draft around the order in which a curious reader could discover the idea. Try reading the last sentence of one paragraph against the first of the next. What has the reader just learned that makes the next thought worth having? Bring in background when that thought needs it. Preserve a good existing paragraph even if it changes your outline.

Give words to the hardest inference. Walk through one person or system encountering the difficulty, what choices or information are available, and what follows. Let the strongest objection develop far enough to alter the conclusion if it is right. Use a concrete comparison where it helps the reader predict a consequence. Cut the second example once the first has done its work.

Write in the author's actual degree of certainty. Use first person for a belief, doubt, or change of mind supported by the material. Never invent an anecdote, acquaintance, former belief, or consensus to supply intimacy. A plain declarative passage can carry an inquiry too.

Read for the moment the reader is asked to admire the writing, accept a verdict on trust, or decode a label. Try deleting that line. If the thought falls apart, supply the missing reason. If it survives, leave the line out. Keep rhythm, humor, technical terms, and headings wherever they earn their space. Read the ending aloud in the voice of the opening. Where the material supports an ordinary action, name who can do it and what they would look for. Listen for a sudden drift into the vocabulary of a report. Stop when the reader can carry the thought forward.

Before delivery, test the hinge as a skeptical reader. Could the same evidence arise for another reason? Are training, execution, measurement, and interpretation being confused? Check load-bearing factual claims against the supplied evidence or primary sources as appropriate. Mark assumptions and qualify what remains uncertain. On revision, fix the stumble locally and reread its consequences downstream. Deliver the requested piece in full, with any working notes kept out of the author's prose.
